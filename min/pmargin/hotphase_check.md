# Hot-phase length experiment on 1SNO — how long does tempering actually need?

Question (follow-up from `packed_1sno_check.md`): the seed-3.0 domain-1
trap (D1 off ~8.7, D2 folded ~2.7) survived tempering because only ~2
effective swap steps fit in the noisy window (frames < 1200, swaps every
500). Does a longer swap window rescue it?

Variants (all identical to `packed_1sno_temper.ergo` except swaps are
allowed only while `FRAME <= SWAP_UNTIL`; generated by
`gen_packed_ww.py`'s hotphase section, files `hotphase_w*.ergo`):
SWAP_UNTIL ∈ {1200, 6000, 12000, 24000, 38400 (quench)}, plus
`hotphase_w38400_hot3.ergo` (full window, LADDER_MAX = 3.0).
Everything else — noise schedule (heat cycles < 1200, baseline 0.001 to
38400, quench 0), ladder 1.0→2.0 geometric, swaps every 500 — unchanged.
Cost: 54 s per variant, deterministic.

**The domain-1 trap is NEVER rescued — not at any swap-window length up
to the quench frame, and not with a 3.0 ladder top.** D1 sits at 8.7–8.8
in every variant. Note also the D2 regression seen in the first temper
run persists in every window (2.68 → ~6.5): swaps move the block's
temperature, the trajectory chaotically reroutes, and D2 lands in a
worse spot regardless of window.

unfavorable Δ).

## The measured answer

**"How long does the hot phase need to be?" is the wrong knob: the
limiting factor is the NOISE FLOOR, not the swap count.** After frame
1200 the schedule's noise amplitude is 0.001 (×TMUL ≤ 3 = 0.003), so
extending the swap window from 2 to 76 swap steps adds Metropolis
activity with essentially no thermal exploration to Metropolis-over.
The trap's RMSD is 9.99 ± 0.01 across a 32× range of window lengths —
flat. To give tempering a real chance at a 98-residue domain re-melt,
the schedule itself must sustain heat (raise HEAT_START or extend the
heat/cool cycles across the window), i.e. a SCHEDULE change, not a
swap-window change. That is the next experiment: HEAT_START 0.001 →
~0.01–0.02 sustained to frame 24000 with swaps every 500, watching (a)
whether block 4's D1 re-melts, (b) whether the good folders survive the
hotter baseline. The hot3 result already warns that too much effective
temperature just degrades the marginal blocks (mean 3.60 → 3.86).

Cost note: all variants cost the same 54 s as the base temper run —
swap steps are ~2 µs of energy evaluations; window length is free.
