# Quaternion winding-sum path integral on S³ — validation findings

Task: validate the winding-sum (sum over classical path windings)
construction of the free-particle propagator on S³ against the
independent exact spectral reference. R=m=1, T̃ = ħT/2, T̃ ∈ {0.1, 0.25,
0.5}, Θ ∈ (0.05, 2π−0.05), 2000-pt grid. Reproduce:

```
python min/quaternion/analyze_prop.py
```

(compiles + runs `winding_s3.ergo` twice — byte-identical; regenerates
`spectral_ref.py`'s reference twice — byte-identical; oracle numbers +
`prop_*.png` plots.)

## Verdict

**VALIDATED — after correcting two normalization errors in the
construction as literally specified.** The corrected identity holds at
the f64 noise floor (max rel error ~5e-13 pointwise, phase constant to
1e-14 rad). The two corrections, found via Poisson resummation and
confirmed numerically (not tuned to match):

1. **Action exponent**: with T̃ = ħT/2, S/ħ = d²/(2ħT) = d²/(4T̃), so
   the phase is `exp(i Θ_n²/(4T̃))`, not the specified `/(2T̃)`.
2. **Prefactor**: the 3D Van Vleck prefactor `(4πiT̃)^(-3/2)`
   (= `(2πiħT/m)^(-3/2)`), not `1/(2π²)` — the 2π² is the S³ area and
   belongs to the spectral side only.

The validated identity (Poisson-exact for every damping ε > 0):

```
(4πiTc)^(-3/2) Σ_n (Θ_n/sin Θ_n) exp(i Θ_n²/(4Tc))  =  e^{-iTc} K_spec(Θ,Tc)
   Θ_n = Θ + 2πn,  Tc = T̃ - iε
```

At real time: **K_wind = e^{−iT̃} K_spec** — the curvature/measure phase
is **−T̃** (negative sign; l(l+2) = (l+1)²−1 ⇒ e^{+iT̃} on the spectral
side).

The plan-as-specified formula (1/2π², /2T̃, undamped) fails
quantitatively, not at a floor: |K_wind/K_spec| spans 1.1–928 over the
probe grid and *grows* with N (undamped partial sums diverge termwise,
|term| ~ n). See the RAW section of the binary output.

## The iε prescription (required)

Both series are divergent-but-oscillatory at real time (terms do not
tend to zero). Measured: undamped spectral sum's relative change vs L
stays ~1e6 from L=250 to L=4000 (no convergence); with ε=0.01 the
change is 0 to machine precision by L=500. The identity is exact at
every ε: probe (Θ=2, T̃=0.25) gives |W/S| = e^{−ε} and arg = −T̃ to 10
decimals for ε ∈ {0.05, 0.02, 0.01, 0.005, 0.002} — i.e. the identity
carries the full complex phase e^{−iTc} (|e^{−iTc}| = e^{−ε}).

## Oracle results

1. **Magnitude match** (vs e^{−iTc}K_spec, full 2000-pt grid):
   max rel err / median rel err —
   T̃=0.1: 2.1e-13 / 1.9e-15 (converged already at N=4);
   T̃=0.25: 4.6e-13 / 3.7e-15 (converged at N=8);
   T̃=0.5: N=4 → 3.0e-2, N=8 → 2.7e-10, N=16,32 → 8.0e-13 / 5.2e-15.
   The N-convergence floor is f64 noise; errors localize at the
   caustics Θ=0, π, 2π (see prop_err_theta.png). Spectral Lmax: exact
   by L=500 at ε=0.01; undamped never converges (above).

2. **Global phase**: arg(K_wind/K_spec) = −T̃ with circular std
   2.5e-15 / 9.6e-15 / 2.1e-14 for T̃ = 0.1 / 0.25 / 0.5 (masked
   |S|>5%·max). Constant phase, sign **negative** — measured, not tuned.

3. **Antipode caustic** (Θ = π±ε_θ, ε_θ = 1e-2…1e-6, N=32, paired
   truncation): the limit exists, is finite, and matches the spectral
   value ((l+1)²(−1)ˡ limit) with rel error scaling ∝ ε_θ²
   (e.g. T̃=0.25: 2.7e-3 → 2.7e-9 → 4.5e-10 floor) — direct evidence of
   the n↔−n−1 pairwise cancellation. At ε=0.01 damping the unpaired
   leftover term is exponentially suppressed (closed form ~1e-174), so
   the unpaired truncation *looks* fine; the weak-damping supplement
   (ε=1e-4) shows the pairing becoming load-bearing: at ε_θ=1e-6 paired
   rel err 1.3e-6 vs unpaired 1.5e-3 and diverging like 1/ε_θ.
   Conclusion: pair-symmetric truncation n∈[−N, N−1] is the correct
   definition; [−N, N] is structurally wrong at the caustic.

4. **Sign structure**: the (+1) weighting matches the scalar
   (integer-l) kernel (median rel err ~2e-15); the (−1)ⁿ weighting
   fails against it (median rel err ~1.2–1.4, O(1) mismatch) — and
   instead matches the **half-integer-momentum (spinor) kernel**
   k = l+1 = m+½ to 2e-15 with the same e^{−iTc} phase. The
   construction's sign choice is the spin-structure choice; the scalar
   propagator selects +1.

5. **Determinism**: Ergo binary twice byte-identical; spectral
   reference regenerated byte-identical; analyzer output identical
   across runs.

6. **Fringe scaling (exploratory)**: |K|² fringe spacing near the
   antipode ≈ 2T̃ (two-path interference prediction) to within ~5% for
   T̃ ≤ 0.15 (ratios 1.02–1.06); at T̃ ≥ 0.25 the two-path picture
   breaks down (multi-winding contributions; fitted exponent over the
   small-T̃ subset: 0.89). Reported as data, no pass/fail.

## Honest boundary

This validates the S³ free-particle propagator construction (quaternion
winding sum = spectral kernel, up to the e^{−iT̃} curvature phase). It
says nothing about the brachistochrone/cycloid mapping from the chat —
the classical cycloid side was the hopfion test (min/hopfion/).

# Spinor extension — the double-cover signature

Extension of the validated S³ study: same kernels, same iε prescription,
paired truncation [−N, N−1]. New binary sections W4S/W4P (scalar/spinor
winding sums on the 2π-shifted grid, plus T̃=π/2 grid-1 sections);
`spectral_ref.py` gains `k_spec_spinor` (half-integer momenta
k = m + ½); `analyze_spinor.py` drives the oracles. Regression: all
prior output sections of the binary are byte-identical, and
`analyze_prop.py` stdout is unchanged.

## Antiperiodicity oracle (the gate) — PASS

Pointwise ratios K(Θ+2π)/K(Θ) on the matched 2000-pt grids, masked
|K| > 1%·max, N=16, T̃ ∈ {0.1, 0.25, 0.5}:

| side | ratio | median dev | p95 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| winding scalar | +1 | 1.3–1.9e-15 | ≤2.7e-14 | ≤4.5e-13 |
| winding spinor | −1 | 4.9–21.5e-15 | ≤2.2e-13 | ≤7.3e-13 |
| spectral scalar | +1 | ~5e-15 | — | ≤1.2e-12 |
| spectral spinor | −1 | ~1–2e-14 | — | ≤7.3e-13 |

Winding-vs-spectral agreement on the shifted grid: max rel err
2.1e-13 / 7.0e-13 (scalar), 2.5e-15 / 1.0e-15 (spinor). Construction
topology == spectrum topology, both covers.

**The measured double-cover signature, in one sentence:** going once
around the classical path (Θ → Θ+2π) leaves the scalar propagator
invariant and flips the spinor propagator's sign — K_s(Θ+2π) = +K_s(Θ),
K_sp(Θ+2π) = −K_sp(Θ) — pointwise, to truncation noise, identically in
the winding-sum construction and in the energy spectrum.

## DeepSeek claims audit

- **Berry-π at the antipode — SURVIVES (it is a parity theorem, and the
  numbers show it exactly).** K_s is symmetric and K_sp antisymmetric
  under δ ↦ −δ about Θ=π (each half-integer-k term is odd), so
  arg(K_sp/K_s) flips by exactly π across the antipode. Measured
  flanking-window phase differences: |flip − π| = 0–4.4e-16 at
  T̃ = 0.1/0.25/0.5 and 1.7e-10 at T̃=π/2 (revival), where the flanking
  phases are ±π/2 (±1.5944/−1.5472). Caveat measured, not assumed: at
  T̃=π/2 the spinor amplitude near the antipode is suppressed 4.45e-04
  (vs 2.9e-02 at the other T̃) — the flip there is between near-zero
  amplitudes.
- **Fringe shift — SURVIVES (exactly half a fringe).** |K|² fringe
  peaks near the antipode, scalar vs spinor: T̃=0.1: shift
  0.102–0.109 vs half the fringe spacing 0.104; T̃=0.25: 0.26–0.30 vs
  0.282. Mechanism is exact: the n=−1 winding carries a (−1) sign in
  the spinor sum, swapping constructive ↔ destructive interference.
  Note |K_sp|² itself is 2π-periodic (the sign squares away); the
  signature lives in the *positions* of the fringes.
- **Cycloid mapping — NULL, as expected.** Under Θ := x/a the cycloid
  cusp x=2πa lands on Θ=2π, which is an S³ conjugate point by shared
  2π periodicity alone (no dynamics). The S³ antipode caustic (Θ=π)
  maps to θ=π — the cycloid's smooth arch bottom, not a caustic. And
  the kernel's fringe structure moves with T̃ (spacing ≈ 2T̃), while any
  cycloid-mapped feature is T̃-invariant — no dynamical alignment beyond
  coincidence.

## Revival spectra (data, no claims)

|K_s|² and |K_sp|² at Θ=π/3 over T̃ ∈ (0, 4π), from the validated
kernels (`spinor_revival.png`): both reach max |K|² = 0.759 but at
different times (scalar T̃=10.88, spinor T̃=1.88); both ≈ 0 at T̃=π/2
(0.0000 / 0.0008). The two statistics give interferometrically distinct
revival patterns — the measurable contrast between covers.
